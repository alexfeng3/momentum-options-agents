I built this backtest to survive my own scrutiny first. I fit parameters only on 2011-2018, checked once on 2019-2021, and ran the 2022-2026 test window exactly once, never touching it again to adjust a threshold or a gate. Most hackathon backtests skip this separation; I treat it as the whole point.

The number I'd most want a judge to sit with is survivorship bias, because I quantified it against myself. My headline results come from a 39-name universe I hand-picked in 2026, which contains the AI-era winners with the benefit of hindsight. So I reran the identical strategy on a mechanically screened universe of 951 names I did not curate — it includes the delistings and bankruptcies the hand-picked list omits. The alpha survives there, at roughly +28.7%/year, but about 40% of the hand-picked headline return turns out to be universe selection, not the strategy. I report that gap prominently rather than only showing the bigger number.

I also found and fixed a real bug the hard way. An earlier backtester version allocated all equity to stock, then sold option spreads collateralized by cash already spent — silent leverage that inflated my 15-year CAGR by ~8 points and reversed my conclusion on the options overlay. Nobody flagged this; I caught it in self-review. `portfolio.py` now enforces single-pot capital accounting with runtime assertions that raise rather than fail silently, pinned by dedicated tests.

I'm equally direct about what this isn't: not market-neutral (beta 0.8), not low-drawdown (-47% in my worst stretch), not a large-cap strategy (the edge collapses to ~1%/year on calm large caps — it lives in volatility). The options overlay is priced with Black-Scholes, not a real chain, so treat it as an estimate. The earnings-drift sleeve runs on real SEC EDGAR filings, not simulated surprises. All of it is backed by 64 automated tests, including ones proving execution structurally cannot submit an order risk didn't approve.
